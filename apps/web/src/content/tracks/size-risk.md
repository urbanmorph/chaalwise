idea: 'Commit in proportion to your edge, how far the odds favour you. Your edge is only a guess, so commit less than it suggests, and no single loss can ruin you.'
why: 'Even good chances fail sometimes. Whoever risks everything on one good chance is wiped out by bad luck sooner or later. Whoever risks a sensible share stays in long enough for the edge to pay off. For a one-off, large decision the rule is simpler: never risk ruin.'
atWork: 'Pilot the new product with one region or one customer group before you move the whole budget to it.'
inStudy: 'Do not stake the whole grade on one ambitious project; keep the core work safe and take the risk where a miss costs little.'
habit: 'If this goes wrong, can I recover, and is what I risk worth the edge?'
