---
idea: 'Work a plan through, step by step, to the end before you commit to it.'
why: 'A plan that looks good for two steps can fail on the third. Counting the sequence to the end, as a chess player counts captures, turns a hopeful plan into a known one, and shows the step where it breaks. Where the later steps cannot be foreseen, commit in stages and count again as you learn.'
atWork: 'Before agreeing a delivery date, walk through each hand-off: who does what, what it waits for, and the step most likely to slip.'
inStudy: 'In a maths or science problem, write each step and check it before the next, instead of jumping to the answer you expect.'
habit: 'If I do this, then what, and then what, until it settles?'
