Poker here means Texas hold'em. Each player gets two cards of their own, face down. Five shared cards are dealt face up in the middle, for everyone. Your hand is the best five cards from your two and the five shared, in any mix.

## The chart, from best to worst

|     | Hand            | What it is                                                       | Example          |
| --- | --------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------- |
| 1   | Straight flush  | Five cards in a row, all of one suit. Ace high is a royal flush. | A♥ K♥ Q♥ J♥ 10♥  |
| 2   | Four of a kind  | Four cards of one rank.                                          | 9♠ 9♥ 9♦ 9♣ K♠   |
| 3   | Full house      | Three cards of one rank and two of another.                      | Q♠ Q♥ Q♦ 4♣ 4♠   |
| 4   | Flush           | Any five cards of one suit.                                      | K♦ J♦ 8♦ 5♦ 2♦   |
| 5   | Straight        | Five ranks in a row, of any suits. The ace counts high or low.   | 9♣ 8♦ 7♠ 6♥ 5♣   |
| 6   | Three of a kind | Three cards of one rank.                                         | 7♠ 7♥ 7♦ K♣ 2♠   |
| 7   | Two pair        | Two different pairs.                                             | J♠ J♥ 4♦ 4♣ A♠   |
| 8   | One pair        | Two cards of one rank.                                           | 10♠ 10♥ A♦ 8♣ 3♠ |
| 9   | High card       | None of the above, so your highest card counts.                  | A♠ J♦ 8♣ 6♥ 3♠   |

Any hand in the chart beats every hand below it. So a flush beats a straight, and a straight beats three of a kind.

## When two hands are the same kind

The higher cards that make the hand win: a pair of kings beats a pair of queens, and three queens beat three fours. If those are equal, compare the other cards from the top down. These are called kickers. If all five cards match in rank, the players split the pot. Suits never decide.

## Why this order

The order comes from how rare each hand is among five-card hands. A flush, for example, turns up about half as often as a straight, so it ranks higher.

With seven cards to choose from, as in Texas hold'em, the frequencies shift. No pair at all (about 17% of seven-card hands) becomes rarer than one pair (44%) or two pair (23%). The order stays the same anyway, by convention.
