Say you hold four cards of one suit and need a fifth for a flush. Nine cards of that suit are still unseen, so you have nine outs. For a quick estimate of your chance, multiply your outs by four when two cards are still to come, or by two when one card is to come. Nine outs give about 36% by the rule, against 35% worked out exactly.

The rule of four works for about eight outs or fewer, and only when no more betting can come before the last card. With 15 outs it says 60%; the real chance is 54%.

Count carefully: an out that also helps the other player is not really yours.
