faq:
  - q: 'What is equity in poker?'
    a: 'Equity is your share of the pot on average: how often your hand ends up best if the rest of the cards were dealt many times. A tie counts as half. A hand that wins 60 times in 100 has 60% equity.'
  - q: 'How do you work out your equity?'
    a: 'With one card to come against a known hand, count the cards that win for you and divide by the unseen cards. King and queen of diamonds against a pair of aces, on K♥ 7♣ 2♦ 9♠, wins with a king or a queen: 5 of the 44 unseen cards, about 11%. With more cards to come, an equity calculator deals out every case.'
  - q: 'How do you find your equity against a range?'
    a: 'Average your equity against each hand they could hold, weighted by how often they would hold it. With the same cards, against a pair of aces (11%) or king and jack (93%), each as likely, your equity is about 52%.'
  - q: 'How do you use equity to decide whether to call?'
    a: 'Compare it with the pot odds. Calling 10 into a pot of 30 puts in 10 of the 40 you could win, so you need more than 25% equity. With 11% you fold; with 52% you call.'
---

If your hand wins 60 times in 100 against the other hand, you have 60% equity. Equity says nothing about any single hand. It is what you would get on average if the same situation were played many times.
