---
title: The rushed website
summary: A big client wants a website sooner than your team can easily make it. Count the cost, guard against late changes, protect your lead, and weigh a claim about price.
game: case
stage: 3
order: 2
skills: [calculate, think-ahead, size-risk, bluff]
steps:
  - kind: read
    id: intro
    text: You run a team of five at a small design studio. A large client wants a new website in eight weeks, with a bonus of ₹1 lakh if you make it. Your team usually needs ten weeks for a job this size.
  - ask: cases/rushed-website/rushed-offer
  - kind: read
    id: counted
    text: You count it through. The overtime would cost ₹1.6 lakh, more than the bonus. You offer nine weeks with no bonus, and the client agrees. One worry is left. On past jobs, the client's marketing head has changed the brief late, after the work was done.
  - ask: cases/rushed-website/late-changes
  - kind: read
    id: ahead
    text: The sign-offs work. By week seven you are a week ahead. Then the client asks for an extra, moving pictures on the home page, “if there is time”. It is a kind of work your team has not built before.
  - ask: cases/rushed-website/extra-feature
  - kind: read
    id: next-job
    text: You launch on time, and the client is pleased. A month later they ask for a price on the next job, and add that other studios have quoted 20% less than you. They do not show the quotes.
  - ask: cases/rushed-website/lower-quote
  - kind: plan
    id: plan
    prompt: Use it this week. When will you count it through?
    situations:
      - someone offers a reward for doing a job faster
      - I am about to agree to a deadline
      - an offer sounds good before I have counted it
    action: I will count what it takes, week by week, before I say yes
---

A story in four decisions, for people who run a team. It uses [Count it through](/skills/count-it-through/), [Not so fast](/skills/not-so-fast/), [Simplify when ahead](/skills/simplify-when-ahead/) and the [Bluff and model opponents](/skills/bluff/) course.
