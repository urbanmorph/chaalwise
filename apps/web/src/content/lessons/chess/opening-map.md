    text: Openings are grouped by their first moves. The first pawn moves decide where the pawns will stand, and that decides the kind of game. Open games start 1.e4 e5. Lines open early, pieces come out fast, and tactics, short sequences of moves that win something, come soon. The Italian, the Ruy Lopez and the Scotch are open games.

    id: semi-open
    text: Semi-open games start 1.e4 and any Black reply except ...e5. Black avoids copying White with 1...e5, so the pawns end up in a different shape. The game is sharp, full of risk for both sides, in the Sicilian. It is solid and calmer in the Caro-Kann and the French. The Sicilian (1...c5), the French (1...e6), the Caro-Kann (1...c6) and the Scandinavian (1...d5) are semi-open.
    position: 'moves: e4 c5'

  - kind: read
    id: closed
    text: Closed games start 1.d4 d5. This map also puts here the other replies to 1.d4, except ...Nf6, such as the Dutch (1...f5). The play is slower. Players move pieces patiently to better squares and fight over where the pawns stand. The Queen's Gambit and the Slav are closed games, and the London System is a way for White to play them.
    position: 'moves: d4 d5'
    highlight: [d4, d5]
  - kind: read
    id: indian
    text: Indian games start 1.d4 Nf6. Black lets White take the centre with pawns, then attacks it with pieces, and with pawn moves that challenge White's pawns. The King's Indian, the Nimzo-Indian and the Grünfeld are Indian games.
    position: 'moves: d4 Nf6'
    highlight: [d4, f6]

    id: flank
    text: Flank openings start 1.c4, 1.Nf3 or 1.g3. White controls the centre from the side, or flank, and often reaches the other families by another road. The English (1.c4) and the Réti (1.Nf3) are flank openings.

There are hundreds of named openings, but only a few families. Knowing the family tells you what kind of game to expect before you know the name.
