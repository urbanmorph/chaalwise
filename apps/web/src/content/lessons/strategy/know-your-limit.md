    text: "What both share has a name: the winner's curse. When several people bid for something worth the same to all of them, and each can only guess its worth, the winner is usually the one who guessed furthest out. That means too high in an auction, and too low in a quote. Capen, Clapp and Campbell (1971) saw it in oil firms bidding for the right to drill. So before you bid, decide what it is worth to you. When everyone is guessing the same value, bid below your guess, and further below the more rivals there are. Set your walk-away point too, as in the Split the pie lab: the price beyond which no deal is better. Then keep to it, however the bidding goes."
