---
title: Their next move
summary: Think through how the other side will respond, as they see it, not as you hope.
game: strategy
stage: 1
order: 2
skills: [think-ahead]
after: chess/blunder-check
steps:
  - kind: read
    id: on-the-board
    text: On the board, when you consider a move you ask, if I play this, what will they play? Their best reply, not the one you hope for. Here, if Black plays ...g6 to chase the queen, the queen simply takes on e5 with check.
    position: 'moves: e4 e5 Qh5'
    highlight: [h5, e5]
  - kind: read
    id: off-the-board
    text: Off the board, before you act, think through how the other side will respond. Picture their best reply from where they stand, then decide.
  - kind: choice
    id: ask-for-a-raise
    prompt: You plan to ask for a raise the week after missing an important deadline. What is your manager's most likely reply?
    options:
      - They bring up the missed deadline
      - They agree at once
      - They ignore the request
    answer: 0
    explanation: Their best reply uses your weakest point. Deal with that first, or choose a better moment.
  - kind: choice
    id: price-cut
    prompt: You cut your shop's prices by ten per cent to win customers. What does the shop across the road most likely do?
    options:
      - Cut its prices too, so neither of you gains much
      - Leave its prices alone
      - Close down
    answer: 0
    explanation: If their best reply cancels your move, your move may only cost you. Think one reply ahead before you commit.
  - kind: read
    id: try-it
    text: Try it this week. Before a conversation that matters, write down the other person's best reply, and how you would answer it.
---

Chess players call it anticipation. In daily life it is just thinking one step ahead of the other person.
