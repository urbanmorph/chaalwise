In poker, bluffing is part of the game, and a bluff works only when its story is believable. Away from the table, the useful lesson is the other side of it: test the stories others tell, and make your own hold up. The economist Michael Spence (1973) showed why costly signals are believed. The reference entry [Believe what is costly to fake](/reference/strategy/costly-signals/) tells more.
