Poker players learn to explain a move by the cards and the price before they explain it by the player. Off the table the same habit is rare. In a study by Edward Jones and Victor Harris (1967), people read an essay and judged the writer's own views from it. They did so even when told the writer had been given the side to argue. Carey Morewedge and colleagues (2015) trained this error with a game: players made it, then had it named and got feedback. It became less common, and the effect lasted two months or more.
