  - kind: read
    id: on-the-board
    text: On the board, before every move, you look at checks, captures and threats, theirs first. Here the knight on c3 attacks the queen. Missing that is how most games are lost.
    position: 4k3/8/8/8/8/2n5/8/3QK3 w - - 0 1
    highlight: [c3, d1]
  - kind: read
    id: off-the-board
    text: Off the board it is the same habit. Before you act, ask what could go wrong. Look first at what the other side, or the situation, can do to you, and only then at what you want to do.
  - kind: choice
    id: angry-email
    prompt: You are about to send a sharp email to a colleague. What is the blunder check?
    options:
      - Ask how they could respond, and what happens if they forward it
      - Check the spelling
      - Send it before you change your mind
    answer: 0
    explanation: Their reply is the threat. Spelling is the move you wanted to make anyway; the blunder check is about what they can do next.
  - kind: choice
    id: phone-plan
    prompt: A phone plan has a very low price for the first year. What is the threat to look for?
    options:
      - The price after the first year, and the cost of leaving
      - Which colour of phone to choose
      - How fast it is delivered
    answer: 0
    explanation: Like a piece left hanging, the danger is in the part you are not looking at. Check what happens after the offer ends.
  - kind: read
    id: try-it
    text: Try it this week. Before one decision that matters, pause and name one thing that could go wrong, and what you would do about it.
---

The blunder check is the most useful habit in chess. It works off the board too.
